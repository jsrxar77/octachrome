Octachrome

A groovebox you play with a Maschine MK3.

What it is

A groovebox for people who like to play hardware: eight tracks, steps that drift against each other, a harmony that follows the song, and eight instruments. It runs on a small computer and is played on a Native Instruments Maschine MK3.

The two screens of the MK3 as Octachrome draws them, taken from the real program. The left one is time, the right one is sound.

Three things that make it different

Every track has its own length

A bar of seven steps against a bar of sixteen drifts apart and comes back together by itself. Nobody has to program the variation.

A step is more than a note

Hold a pad and make a step repeat, play only sometimes, play every second time or play late. Each track also has its own effect and its own LFO.

A brain that knows chords

Tracks connected to the Brain follow a chord progression, so a bass line written in one key plays in tune with the harmony, bar after bar.

What you need

A Maschine MK3

The only control surface: its pads, knobs, Smart Strip, lights and both screens are used. Octachrome talks to it directly; no other software is needed.

A small computer

A Raspberry Pi 5 (4 GB, with its official power supply) is the target, and a Pi 4 should do. On a Mac you can run it today, and without any hardware a simulator plays the whole thing.

Speakers or headphones

On the MK3's own outputs. Samples you record come in through its input, or from Octachrome itself.

Make a beat

Click steps. Press play. Every row has its own length.

The pads change what they are

Eight knobs, eight colours

Each screen shows what the knob under it does.

Themes change the whole page. On the MK3: SETTINGS, then turn the 4D encoder.

Three themes

Soft, bright, monochrome. Matte is where it starts.

Instruments

At most eight knobs. A drawing that shows the sound. A MIDI out drives an outside synth, and a granular instrument is coming.
